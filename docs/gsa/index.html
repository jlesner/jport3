Running for the CS GSA council, 2026–27

I’m running for Outreach Coordinator, Secretary and/or the Undergraduate Committee of the UCSB Computer Science Graduate Student Association (CS GSA). My statement:

I'm Jay Lesner, a first-year CS PhD student in Xifeng Yan's lab. I did my master's here, so I've been part of the department since 2024, and I spent a year as Graduate Officer for Women in CS (WiCS). I'm on a fellowship with no TA duties, so I have the time to actually show up, run events and help keep the GSA active. In my experience, including guest lecturing in a Game AI course at UC Santa Cruz, undergrads really enjoy hearing about and discussing research, so I'd like to set up talks where grad students share their work with undergrad clubs, plus practice sessions where we dry-run conference talks, quals and defenses for friendly feedback. I'd also like to gauge interest in paper discussion groups, within CS or with other departments.

Why me

  • I’ve done this kind of work. I spent a year as Graduate Officer for Women in Computer Science (WiCS), and I have connections with student groups that I’d like to build on.
  • I know the department. I’ve been in UCSB CS since starting my M.S. here in 2024, so I know its people, its clubs and how things get done.
  • I have the time to show up. As a first-year Ph.D. student on a fellowship, with no TA duties, I can actually show up, run events and help keep the GSA active, and I’ll be here for years.
  • Undergrads enjoy hearing about research. Dr. Daniel Shapiro invites me back to guest lecture in his Game AI course at UC Santa Cruz, where I talk about the messy middle of research, and I’ve presented my own work at ECAI-2024, PAIS-2024 and IEEE EMBC 2026.
  • I follow through. SnipDue went from a hackathon weekend to a live app that won “Best Use of GenAI” at SB Hacks XI, and every project on this site has its paper, slides or demo one click away.

What I’d do

  1. Research talks for undergrad clubs. Grad students share their work with undergrad clubs. Undergrads get an early look at research, and many of them go on to our master’s program; we get low-pressure presentation practice.
  2. Practice talk sessions. A regular slot for dry runs of conference talks, quals and defenses, with friendly feedback. All it needs is us and a room.
  3. Paper discussion groups. I’d gauge interest in reading groups, within CS or together with other departments.
